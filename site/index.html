What this is

A random point generator. You set a radius, it picks a place you have no reason to go to, and then you go there and look at it properly.

The method

Forty-two thousand points are scattered at random through a ring around you. The ring is divided into a grid, and each grid square is counted. Because the scatter is uniform, the number of points in a square has a known average and a known spread, so a square holding far more or far fewer points than expected is a real statistical outlier, not a guess.

Attractor returns the square with the largest surplus. Void returns the square with the largest deficit. Random returns an ordinary point from the scatter, with no test applied at all. Blind spot returns a high-surplus square that is as far as possible from anywhere already in your journal.

The sigma figure is the standard score of the chosen square: how many standard deviations it sits from the expected count. Anything past about three is genuinely unusual for a uniform scatter.

Known places

Separately, the app asks Wikipedia which geotagged articles exist inside your radius and keeps them on this device. That is a decent working definition of a place somebody thought worth writing down: 500 of them sit within three kilometres of central Edinburgh, and about eleven sit within eight kilometres of a Highland glen.

Those places are then folded into the same arithmetic. Nearby is an attractor restricted to squares within three hundred metres of a real place, so the clump is a clump and also somewhere you can stand. Landmark returns a named place directly, weighted towards places the scatter runs hot around. Every other mode reports the nearest known place to the point it picked.

The list is cached in IndexedDB for a day, so a second look at the same area costs nothing. Clearing it is in the places panel.

What it is not

Nothing here is quantum, and nothing here is a measurement of anything outside the arithmetic. The randomness comes from your browser's cryptographic generator, which is seeded by your operating system and is, for every purpose this app has, indistinguishable from any other good source.

The anomaly is an anomaly in the sample. It is a property of the numbers, not of the place. A clump of points is not a vortex, a ley line or a signal. What is real is the thing that happens when a person walks to a coordinate they did not choose and pays attention on the way.

Privacy

No account, no analytics, no cookies, no server-side code. Everything is computed in this tab. Three things leave the device, and only when the app needs them: map tiles for the area you are looking at, the coordinate you search for known places, and the coordinate of a point you ask to have named.

The journal and the place cache live in this browser's storage. Clearing your browser data deletes them. Export the journal if you care about it.

Map data

Tiles by OpenStreetMap contributors, inverted to a night palette in the browser with a CSS filter. No map key, no map account, no per-view billing. Place names and known places via Wikipedia GeoSearch and Nominatim.
